# Predict the Machine — validation plan (pre-registered)

Written on 26 September 2026, **before** any data exists. The thresholds and
the decision date below are fixed now so the results can't quietly move the
goalposts. If we change a rule, we write down the change, the date and why,
next to the original.

**Decision date: Friday 18 December 2026.** On that date we pick one route
(schools or university), or stop product work, using only the rules below.

## What we are testing

The bet: *predict → verify → find where your model broke → one layer deeper*
teaches fixed-width integer arithmetic better than a plain checker, and
teachers or instructors will put it in front of students without being chased.

Two routes, one engine:

- **Schools:** OCR J277 1.2.4 (binary addition, overflow, shifts) and A-level
  two's complement. The projector starter, homework sets and the board.
- **University:** CS:APP chapters 2–3. The 12 C/x86-64 cards and paste mode.

## E0 — Do free LLMs already do this? (desk study, week 1)

- **Materials:** 200 student-style wrong attempts with seeded misconceptions,
  generated by `tools/e0/generate.mjs` (binary addition, overflow, shifts,
  two's complement, x86 flags, C conversions). Each has the question, the
  student's working as a student would type it, and the answer key: the
  correct answer, the first wrong column or step, and the misconception.
- **Procedure:** paste each attempt into the **free tier** of ChatGPT, Claude
  and Gemini with default settings, phrased the way a student would ("I got
  this, where did I go wrong?"). Record each full reply.
- **Scoring** (`tools/e0/score.mjs` + a second human rater on 20%): did the
  reply (a) give the right final answer, (b) point to the first wrong column
  or step, (c) name the misconception?
- **Rule:** if free-tier models identify the first wrong column or step in
  ≥ 95% of attempts, we drop "verified" from the schools pitch and compete on
  workflow (assign, mark cell by cell, class misconception map). We keep it for
  the university route only if the gap holds on the x86 items.

## E1 — Teacher pull (schools), from 19 October 2026

- **Sample:** 15 OCR/AQA teachers recruited through Computing at School forums
  and teacher groups, at least 5 of them non-specialists.
- **What they get:** their own link (`?t=<code>`), the projector starter and
  homework set links. Nothing else is asked of them.
- **Pull** (any one counts; kind words don't):
  - unprompted use in weeks 3–6 **after our last contact**, from per-teacher
    link counts in the host's access logs (the `?t=` code; we collect no
    other data);
  - a second teacher in the same department starting without an invitation;
  - the tool appearing in a scheme of work.
- **Rule:** ≥ 6 of 15 still using it in week 6 → continue the schools route.
  3–5 → one more iteration, on the starter only. ≤ 2 → stop the schools route.

## E2 — Instructor pull (university), from 5 October 2026

- **Sample:** 40 named CS:APP-adopting instructors and TAs.
- **Pull:** a link in a spring-term syllabus, lab handout or course page.
- **Rule:** ≥ 4 by 15 December 2026 → continue the university route. ≤ 1 →
  freeze it for 2027. 2–3 → continue outreach into January with one iteration.

## E3 — Does the drill-down teach? (crossover, 2–20 November 2026)

- **Design:** within-student crossover. A *study set* has two families:
  A = binary addition and overflow, B = binary shifts. Each student is
  randomised by the page (not self-chosen) to arm 0 (full diagnosis + Why on
  A, answer-only on B) or arm 1 (the reverse). The arm travels in the student's
  result code.
- **Outcome:** first-attempt accuracy on **unseen transfer items** in a
  *delayed set* taken ≥ 7 days later, where everything is answer-only.
  Primary measure: the paired difference (drill family − answer-only family)
  per student, standardised as d_z. Counterbalancing cancels family difficulty;
  the CSV export supports a model with arm as a factor.
- **Sample:** about 90 students with delayed data (power ≈ 0.8 to detect
  d_z = 0.3 at α = 0.05, two-sided).
- **Rule:** d_z ≥ 0.3 → invest in the drill-down layers. d_z < 0.1 → position
  the product as a marking-time saver and stop building deeper layers.
  0.1–0.3 → inconclusive; replicate before investing.
- **Ethics:** through a university partner, with parental opt-out. We hold no
  pupil data: result codes live only in the teacher's own Classroom or Teams.

## Ongoing learning metric

A permanent 15% of practice items get answer-only feedback (the *holdout*).
We compare first-attempt accuracy on reviews ≥ 7 days after the original miss,
drill vs holdout. This is the only in-product number we treat as evidence of
learning.

## What does not count as evidence

Satisfaction ratings, "I learned a lot", time on site, raw visit counts, stated
preferences ("I'd use more of these"), letters of intent, and practice-session
improvement on near-identical items within one sitting.

## Privacy in the studies

Nothing a learner types leaves their device. Teachers see result codes that
students paste into the teacher's own assignment. The board decodes them in the
teacher's browser. Per-teacher counts come from the static host's access logs
(`?t=` code only). No accounts, names, emails or analytics scripts.
